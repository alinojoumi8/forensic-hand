**Who is asking.** [Name of the organization], [address], [contact for questions].

**What we ask.** We ask you to write your signature several times on sample sheets, over [number]
sessions. Some people will also be asked to copy the signatures of other participants.

**Why.** We test software that helps document examiners. We need real signatures to measure how
well the software works and where it fails.

**What we keep.** Scans and photos of the sample sheets, the date, the pen type, and a code number.
We do not keep your name with the samples. Your name and code number are kept apart, locked, and
seen only by [role].

**What your samples may be used for.** Tick each use that you agree to. You can agree to some and
not others.

- [ ] Testing the software inside our organization (`benchmark`)
- [ ] Setting the software's thresholds or statistical models (`calibrate`)
- [ ] Training a future machine-learning model (`train`)
- [ ] Publishing test results (numbers, never your signature itself) (`publish`)
- [ ] Showing my signature samples in a published report: [yes / no]

**Your choice.** Taking part is voluntary. You can stop at any time. You can withdraw your consent
later by contacting [contact]. We will then stop using your samples in new work. Results that were
already published cannot be recalled, but they never show your name.

**How long.** We keep the samples until [date or rule], then delete them.

**Who else sees the data.** [None / named partners under a written agreement that keeps your choices].

**Risks.** A signature is personal information. We reduce the risk by using code numbers, keeping
names apart, encrypting the files, and limiting access.
